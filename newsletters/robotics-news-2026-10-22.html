Tesla T3 2026 : Optimus entre en production à Fremont, puce AI5 en avril 2027, premières livraisons externes fin 2027
FINANCECompilation de sources publiques · 22/10/2026

Eka Robotics lève 150 M$ en série A à 1,45 Md$ de valorisation, avec une architecture « vision–force–action »
FINANCETechCrunch · 20/10/2026
Fondée en 2025 par Pulkit Agrawal, professeur associé au MIT, et Tuomas Haarnoja, ancien chercheur de Google DeepMind, Eka Robotics a bouclé un tour de série A de 150 millions de dollars (environ 1,0 Md CNY), mené par a16z et F-Prime, avec Samsung Next, Glasswing, Bison, MFV Partners, Obvious Ventures et E14 Fund ; la valorisation post-money atteint 1,45 milliard de dollars, après un amorçage d'environ 11 millions. Le choix technique est le point central : plutôt qu'un modèle VLA (vision-langage-action), Eka développe un modèle VFA (vision-force-action) qui traite le retour de force comme un signal de premier rang au même titre que la vision, en générant des environnements d'apprentissage par renforcement dans un simulateur physique — plusieurs milliers d'heures d'expérience en quelques minutes, selon la société. Côté matériel, la pince à retour de force est annoncée sous 1 000 dollars, pour une durée de vie nominale de 5 millions de cycles (objectif 20 millions). Chiffres publiés : environ 1 500 saisies par heure (contre 600 pour un humain), plus de 95 % de réussite, objets limités à 1 kg. Raghu Raghuram, associé chez a16z, affirme que la société « dépasse déjà la performance humaine sur certaines tâches ».

AGIBOT has open-sourced the fourth instalment of AGIBOT WORLD 2026, the Physical Reasoning dataset, described as the industry’s first real-world Physical Agent dataset. The methodological difference is the evaluation axis: tasks are organised by decision difficulty rather than manipulation difficulty. The example given is “restock to three bottles”, where the robot must first distinguish empty from full bottles, count them, then decide how many to remove and how many to add — a completely different sequence depending on the initial state. The dataset covers four key factors — object quantity, occlusion relationships, spatial occupancy and task progress — and deliberately captures the decision and execution of the same task under different states and constraints, across nine core reasoning capabilities: attribute-constrained selection, context-conditioned selection, precondition reasoning, goal-directed search, long-horizon planning and memory-state maintenance among them, graded Easy, Medium and Hard. The data chain describes the full objective through the task instruction and structures the task via Task Frame, sub-task intervals and action steps; at the action level, a Dense Prompt specifies the target object, the part to manipulate, left/right arm division and expected outcome, paired with real robot motion, along with box, point and line spatial annotations and success or perturbation labels. The dataset is already being used to train AGIBOT’s upcoming agent framework and next-generation foundation model.

A Figure 03 humanoid walked for nine consecutive days on a treadmill, covering about 550 to 560 km in 1.2 million steps. CEO Brett Adcock described it as a “side project” to stress the robot and see what fails. A reading caveat matters: the robot is tethered to mains power in the footage, so this is a mechanical and software endurance test decoupled from battery limits, not nine days on a single charge; Figure has not said whether engineers intervened, nor published the protocol or maintenance logs. It is the second long demonstration this year — a livestreamed parcel-sorting run planned for eight hours lasted nine days and handled 249,560 parcels, but with multiple robots rotating every three to four hours and charging in the gaps. The Figure 03, unveiled last October, carries cameras twice as fast and a quarter of the latency, with 60% wider fields of view, palm cameras in both hands, tactile sensors detecting about 3 grams of force, and inductive charging through the feet at up to 2 kW. The known limitation: a treadmill reproduces neither variable floors, nor unexpected obstacles, nor the frequent stops of a factory or warehouse.

Geek+ releases Gravity Control: out-of-distribution task success rises from 17.5% to 87.5% without retraining or new demonstrations
TECHNOLOGYShanghai Securities News · 2026-10-21
Geek+ has introduced Gravity Control, an embodied-AI model whose premise is not to make the robot “understand a sentence” but to make a correction intent cheaply translatable into verifiable, bounded runtime control inside the model. The method formally defines a “concept property chain”, explores the action representations inside an end-to-end model and builds a concept control table; when the robot deviates, the operator issues a natural-language hint (“grasp deeper and lower on the next pick”), the system queries the table and applies in-line steering to internal activations, intervening directly on already-learned primitive motor representations instead of replanning the whole trajectory; a closed-loop gate caps the maximum intervention dose so that only specific geometric degrees of freedom change without catastrophic divergence. The measured result: on real out-of-distribution tasks, without updating the original policy weights or adding demonstrations, overall success rises from 17.5% to 87.5%, and one individual task from 5% to 95%. The model is not tied to a specific VLA architecture and has been evaluated on π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT, the LIBERO benchmark and physical platforms. The company expects more than 10,000 embodied-AI units shipped over three years.
